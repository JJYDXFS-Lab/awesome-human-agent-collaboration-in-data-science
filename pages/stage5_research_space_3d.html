3D Research Space

Why this view

Papers across research communities approach human-agent collaboration in different ways. This view offers a shared framework for comparing their tasks, human involvement, and agent capabilities.

How we built it

We selected papers for deeper reading, generated structured paper cards from their full text with LLM assistance, and used a common framework to position them in this research space.

About this view: axes, selection, and citations
What is included?
A human-selected deep-analysis subset, including manually added preprints. It is not the entire screened collection.
What do the axes mean?
Application Grounding ranges from generic frameworks to data-science and graph-analysis workflows. Human/User Involvement ranges from autonomy to human guidance, verification, and collaboration. Agentic Mechanism Depth describes mechanisms such as planning, tools, memory, and recovery. These are interpretive positions, not quality rankings.
What do the connections mean?
The citation overlay shows references between papers in this collection, not a complete citation network. Nearby points share positions on the chosen axes; proximity alone does not establish a relationship.
How should I read the yearly views?
They describe this selected set, not field-wide prevalence. Publication metadata is used when verified, with arXiv v1 dates as a fallback.

Interpretive Findings

Benchmark/testbed-heavy, collaboration-light: 48/95 retained papers carry a benchmark/testbed marker (25 primary benchmark/dataset, 13 evaluation testbed, 10 method-with-benchmark). Papers with both high human involvement and high application grounding remain much rarer.
Two literatures are adjacent but not merged: autonomy/data-agent papers have strong application and agentic scores, while HAI/collaboration papers have stronger human scores but weaker data/graph grounding.
Graph is a focused side line: the graph-related set is small (8) and mostly evaluates autonomous graph reasoning or graph-problem solving rather than human-agent graph analysis.
Likely thesis opening: turn benchmark/task infrastructure into selective human-agent collaboration settings: clarification, verification burden, handoff, and recovery are the missing connective tissue.

High Application, Low Human: Autonomy Gap

  1. LLM-Based Multi-Agent Blackboard System for Information Discovery in Data Science
    The main contribution is an autonomous multi-agent data-science method that supplies a coordination substrate and comparison condition for future human-agent collaboration studies.
  2. Data Interpreter: An LLM Agent for Data Science
    Its main contribution is an autonomous data-science agent whose planning and recovery mechanisms provide a baseline and potential substrate for human-agent collaboration. Hierarchical graphs support that agent architecture.
  3. $R^3$DAO: Reactive Recovery and Reconstruction for Long-horizon Data Agent Orchestration
    The main contribution is autonomous data-science orchestration and self-repair. It provides a strong baseline for studying when human guidance, verification, or takeover should augment autonomous recovery. Its orchestration graphs do not make it a graph-analysis paper.
  4. DeepAnalyze: Agentic Large Language Models for Autonomous Data Science
    Provides a concrete autonomous data-science system for comparing where human guidance, verification, and handoff could add value.
  5. DSFlow: Evolutionary Workflow Optimization for Generalizable LLM-Based Data Science Automation
    Its main contribution is autonomous workflow optimization for data science, making it a strong method baseline and substrate for human-agent workflow design.
  6. MA - GTS : A Multi-Agent Framework for Solving Complex Graph Problems in Real-World Applications
    Graph extraction, graph algorithm selection, and graph-theoretic problem solving define the main method contribution. Its autonomous workflow also provides a useful comparison for collaboration research.
  7. Tree-Notebook: A Context-Aware Agent with Tree Search and Entropy-Aware Data Shadow for Interactive Data Science
    Provides an autonomous analytical workflow system and concrete recovery mechanisms that can serve as a baseline or substrate for future human oversight and handoff designs.
  8. A Diagnostic Failure Taxonomy and Trajectory Critic for Data Agent Improvement
    The runtime critic supplies a concrete autonomous failure-recovery system that can serve as a baseline or substrate for human oversight research. The taxonomy and corpus support that contribution.

High Human, Lower Application: Transferable HAI

  1. Learning from Active Human Involvement through Proxy Value Propagation
    Useful as a concrete reference for monitoring, takeover, corrective demonstration, and returning control. Its control-learning setting makes it peripheral evidence for human-agent division of labor in data science.
  2. Substance over Style: Evaluating Proactive Conversational Coaching Agents
    Its main contribution directly concerns mixed-initiative interaction and how agents balance probing with recommendations. This contribution-based color applies despite its peripheral application domain.
  3. Towards Effective and Interpretable Human-Agent Collaboration in MOBA Games: A Communication Perspective
    Its main contribution directly addresses bidirectional human-agent collaboration and selection among human and agent directives. This contribution warrants the collaboration color even though the application remains a boundary reference for the user's thesis.
  4. Leveraging Dual Process Theory in Language Agent Framework for Real-time Simultaneous Human- AI Collaboration
    Its main contribution is a method for simultaneous human-agent coordination under latency and intent uncertainty, making it central collaboration evidence despite the indirect application relevance.
  5. Mapping the Design Space of Teachable Social Media Feed Experiences
    Useful HCI background for non-expert guidance and oversight, but its main contribution concerns teachable feed experiences rather than division of labor in analytical workflows.
  6. Enhancing Human Experience in Human-Agent Collaboration: A Human-Centered Modeling Approach Based on Positive Human Gain
    The main contribution directly models how agents should divide behavior between helping human partners and maintaining their own competence, making it central collaboration-method evidence despite the domain mismatch.
  7. Feedback by Design: Understanding and Overcoming User Feedback Barriers in Conversational Agents
    The main contribution is empirical evidence about human-agent feedback, oversight, and repair. Its background status for this project reflects limited domain specificity rather than peripheral treatment of collaboration.
  8. A call for collaborative intelligence: Why human-agent systems should precede ai autonomy
    Its central claim directly concerns prioritizing human-agent collaboration and designing roles, oversight, and task allocation. The conceptual evaluation guidance supports that collaboration agenda.

Closest to Target Intersection

  1. Dango: A Mixed-Initiative Data Wrangling System using Large Language Model
    The main contribution is mixed-initiative cooperation in data wrangling, with explicit allocation of interpretation and synthesis to agents and disambiguation, verification, and correction to users.
  2. LAPS: Automating Hypothesis-Driven Statistical Analysis of Public Survey Using Large Language Models
    Its main contribution is a mixed-initiative analytical system that allocates proposal generation, execution, and debugging to agents while reserving review, approval, and interpretation for researchers.
  3. TexeraAgent: An AI-Agent for Data Science Using Dataflows
    The main contribution uses shared executable workflows to distribute work between analysts and agents and enable oversight, correction, and recovery. Autonomous evaluation supports the system but does not displace its collaboration focus.
  4. IDA-Bench: Evaluating LLMs on Interactive Guided Data Analysis
    Its central research question concerns agents following evolving analyst guidance within a shared analytical workflow. The benchmark contribution supplies evaluation evidence for that division of labor.
  5. Data Formulator 2: Iterative Creation of Data Visualizations, with AI Transforming Data Along the Way
    The main contribution operationalizes and studies mixed-initiative analytical work: analysts direct and verify, while AI generates and repairs transformations. This substantive fit outweighs the supplied background label.
  6. Collaborative Gym: A Framework for Enabling and Evaluating Human-Agent Collaboration
    Although it contributes evaluation infrastructure, its central research question concerns how humans and agents share control and collaborate effectively. That makes it a collaboration anchor with a benchmark marker.
  7. Are Large Language Models Ready for Multi-Turn Tabular Data Analysis?
    The main contribution is benchmark infrastructure for evaluating conversational analysis agents. Its collaboration patterns and reflection baseline are valuable supporting contributions, but the central evidence concerns evaluation rather than human-agent role allocation in practice.
  8. Cocoa: Co-Planning and Co-Execution with AI Agents
    The main contribution operationalizes mixed-initiative collaboration through shared planning, adjustable delegation, execution oversight, and revision, supported by human studies.

Primary Benchmark / Dataset Papers

  1. A Diagnostic Failure Taxonomy and Trajectory Critic for Data Agent Improvement
    The runtime critic supplies a concrete autonomous failure-recovery system that can serve as a baseline or substrate for human oversight research. The taxonomy and corpus support that contribution.
  2. CoMind: Towards Community-Driven Agents for Machine Learning Engineering
    Its main relevance is as a community-augmented autonomous ML-engineering system and a contrast for human-agent collaboration. MLE-Live warrants the benchmark marker; incidental dependency graphs do not justify a graph-related role.
  3. UniDataBench: Evaluating Data Analytics Agents Across Structured and Unstructured Data
    Its primary contribution is an evaluation benchmark for heterogeneous data analytics agents. ReActInsight also makes it useful as an autonomy baseline, but benchmark design anchors the paper's research role.
  4. Scaling Generalist Data-Analytic Agents
    The main contribution is a training method for autonomous analytical agents. It supplies a capability baseline and methodological substrate for collaborative systems, with the dataset contribution represented separately.
  5. Are Large Language Models Ready for Multi-Turn Tabular Data Analysis?
    The main contribution is benchmark infrastructure for evaluating conversational analysis agents. Its collaboration patterns and reflection baseline are valuable supporting contributions, but the central evidence concerns evaluation rather than human-agent role allocation in practice.
  6. Benchmarking Data Science Agents
    The main contribution is a lifecycle evaluation paradigm and benchmark suite. It supports comparisons with autonomous data science agents, while its primary research role is evaluation infrastructure.
  7. KRAMABENCH: A Benchmark for AI Systems on Data-to-Insight Pipelines over Data Lakes
    Its main contribution is a benchmark and diagnostic evaluation framework for data-science agents. Autonomous systems provide the evaluated subjects and reference implementation.
  8. DAComp: Benchmarking Data Agents across the Full Data Intelligence Lifecycle
    Its main contribution is benchmark and evaluation design for full-lifecycle data agents. It also provides a useful autonomy baseline, but the central research contribution is measuring capability and diagnosing failures.
